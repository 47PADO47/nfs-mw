# Original camera host and render timing

## Scope and evidence

This host integrates the original PC contracts in
[original-camera-follow.md](original-camera-follow.md),
[original-camera-data.md](original-camera-data.md) and
[original-camera-obstruction.md](original-camera-obstruction.md).
Chase, Dynamic FOV, free and orbit controllers keep their existing behavior.

The original main loop obtains scheduler wall time and dispatches that same
elapsed time to camera actions and movers. Speedbreaker scales world simulation,
not this camera clock. The native rolling cubics, acceleration history and
half-weight clearance recurrences depend on the number of camera updates.

## Stable camera clock

For high-frame-rate operation, advance the original controller at 60 updates per
unpaused real second. This is an explicit host adaptation to the original
60-FPS camera behavior, rather than running its recurrences every render frame.
Do not cap or discard long frame deltas. Paused/loading/garage time does not
accumulate work to catch up later.

Keep all body inputs generated by the world's fixed physics steps until camera
updates for that frame consume them. Give them simulation timestamps. For each
camera tick, map its real timestamp through the same Speedbreaker ramp integral
and GameSpeed as the world clock. Sample continuous position, velocity, speed
and body orientation one physics step behind that simulation time, interpolating
between the bracketing recorded states. The fixed delay guarantees the same
bracket exists even when a fast render frame has no new physics step; it also
matches the world's displayed-pose latency. This preserves intermediate physics
steps when a slow render frame advances several ticks. Slowed simulation must
not repeat a final pose across camera ticks and produce artificial acceleration
spikes. Discrete collision events retain their physical timestamps and are
consumed once on the undelayed camera mapping, independently of pose interpolation.

Speedbreaker camera zoom also advances on the camera clock: its retained weight
approaches one at four per real second, and zero at one per real second. Pass
`1 - 0.4 * weight` to the native POV target calculation. Look-back changes the
native horizontal eye/target signs and car visibility gate; it does not borrow
the custom Chase camera's orbit animation.

## Display interpolation and camera lifetime

Retain consecutive camera samples and their anchor positions/bases. Render
interpolated offsets with the car's interpolated geometry anchor, so translating
the car and camera use the same display timeline. For Bumper and Hood, express
offsets in each sampled body basis before interpolation and apply the displayed
body basis; this preserves body attachment through pitch and roll. Outside
views interpolate their already filtered world-space offsets and up vectors.
Presentation sampling must never advance cubic state or collision history.

Mode changes between outside views keep their native follow history. Changes
to/from Bumper or Hood snap on the next camera tick. A respawn, replacement car,
or explicit return from a custom/free camera starts a fresh original controller;
camera samples must never interpolate across that discontinuity. Render camera
cut flags are used for discontinuities, not ordinary outside-to-outside blends.

The player is absent in Bumper and in Hood look-back. Hood normally retains the
car, including its bonnet. Other cars and scenery stay present. Main-view
visibility changes must not remove the player from reflection/shadow inputs.

## Validation boundary

Replay the same physics input keys at 5, 30, 60, 144 and 360 render FPS, both
normally and with quarter-speed simulation. Check retained output at common
camera ticks, interpolated body attachment, no discarded intervals, pause,
view switches, look-back, respawn and Speedbreaker entry/exit. Test the original
projection at 4:3, 16:9 and ultrawide aspects, and cover installed per-car refs.

Native isolated vectors establish arithmetic contracts. They do not by
themselves establish live original-game visual equivalence, world-query
coverage, event producer equivalence or all cinematic/ICE camera behavior.


## Custom camera clearance

The custom Chase and Dynamic FOV controllers retain their own orbit, recenter,
look-back, shake and vertical-FOV behavior. Their unoccluded intent is sampled
with the physical body history; static clearance and avoidable-body elevation
advance at 60 unscaled real updates per second, including Speedbreaker.
They use the outside-view barrier fan and floor policy, without treating road
triangles as walls or imposing the former 2.8 m minimum camera distance.

After interpolating a custom shot onto the displayed body, an immediate,
stateless clearance pass checks the final eye, including Speedbreaker offset
and shake. It uses full required wall/body clearance without advancing retained
history. This is a custom safety policy, not a claim about native interpolation.
Custom vertical FOV and 0.3 m near plane remain unchanged. Native views retain
their original half-weight response and selected reference projection.

Native look-back consumes the held action directly, including visibility/HUD
selection. It must not inherit custom Chase's lingering return swing on release.
The custom controller retains that smooth return behavior.
